**Subject:** Workshop: building the calculation knowledge architecture together

Hi all,

Here is the main architecture of what we discussed in Finland: [link to the architecture page]. I think it is a common vision we can all share. The idea is to retrieve every calculation we own (Excel, Mathcad, Python, notebooks), distil the design logic inside them, and consolidate it into one version-controlled knowledge base. From that knowledge base we produce tools and human-readable calculations, and every project feeds back into it.

**The idea of the workshop** is that each of you picks an area of interest and comes with proposals, solutions, or prototypes that already work for some of the blockers. For each component in your area, please also say how confident you are that you can solve it (low / medium / high), and roughly what it would take.

We will split into four teams, one for each colour on the page, and discuss those modules separately:

| Team | Components | Central question |
|---|---|---|
| **Ingestion** (blue) | Retrieval and curation · Conversion | How do we find, select and read every calculation file reliably? |
| **Distillation** (green) | Decomposition · Consolidation by consensus | How do we turn a file into modular logic, and decide what enters the unified design logic? |
| **Knowledge base** (violet) | Object generation · Repository and search | How do we generate tested calculation objects, host them, and make them findable? |
| **Use and maintenance** (amber) | Self-documenting calculations · Maintenance | How do we produce calculations at the quality of our existing ones, and keep the knowledge base healthy? |

**What we want to achieve in the session:**
1. A short walk through the architecture.
2. Each person states their area, their confidence per component, and shows any prototype.
3. Four breakout teams, one per colour, work on their modules.
4. Each team reports back: proposed solutions or MVPs, confidence, and an owner for the next step.

It would help if you reply before the session with your area of interest, so the teams are balanced.
